---
title: Gave or received tough feedback
---

**What this question tests.** **Tough feedback** is a judgment about someone's work that is hard to hear: a design is flawed, code is not ready, a habit hurts the team. goal.md asks for technical feedback, so a story about code, design, or results fits the role best. Either direction works, and each shows something different:

- **Received** — you take criticism without defending, check whether it is right, and change.
- **Given** — you raise a problem early, specifically, and kindly, and the working relationship survives.

The job description names feedback and team discussion (goal.md). In a consulting team, reviews happen under client deadlines, and a problem nobody raises reaches the client. The interviewer listens for the feedback itself in concrete terms, how you handled your first reaction or theirs, and what changed afterwards.

Pick the direction whose story has the clearer change. Keep a short version of the other direction ready, since a follow-up may ask for it.

**How it is asked:**

- "Tell me about a time you received critical feedback. How did you respond?"
- "Describe a time you had to tell a colleague their work wasn't good enough."
- "When did you disagree with a code review?"

**Questions to find your story:**

- Which review comment or conversation stung, and was it right?
- What did I change because of feedback, in the work or in how I have worked since?
- When did I see a problem in a teammate's work and have to decide whether to say it?
- How did I say it: in private, with examples, with a suggestion?
- How did the working relationship hold up afterwards?

**A strong pick** is technical, the feedback is concrete, and something visibly changed. The interviewer scores you, so a story in which the other person is the villain scores against you.
