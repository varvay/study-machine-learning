---
title: Influenced a team decision
---

**What this question tests.** **Influencing a decision** means changing what a team decides through evidence and persuasion, without the authority to decide it yourself. The job description asks for a subject matter expert who takes part in team discussion, and goal.md reads that as influencing technical direction. In consulting, the same skill faces the client: recommending an option and defending it with its trade-offs. The interviewer listens for how you understood the other views, what evidence you brought, how you handled disagreement, and what happened afterwards.

**How it is asked:**

- "Tell me about a time you convinced your team to take a different approach."
- "Describe a time you disagreed with a technical decision."
- "How have you influenced a decision you didn't own?"

**Questions to find your story:**

- Which decision went differently because of me: a tool, a design, a priority, a scope?
- What did the others want at first, and why was it reasonable to them?
- What evidence did I bring: a prototype, a benchmark, cost figures, a written comparison?
- Did I change my own mind on any point along the way?
- Did the decision prove right afterwards?
- Did I ever lose such an argument and then commit fully to the team's choice? That story counts too.

**A strong pick** has a real disagreement, evidence rather than opinion, and a decision that mattered to the project or the client. A decision about naming or formatting is too small to show influence.
