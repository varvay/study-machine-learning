---
title: Knew when to seek guidance
---

**What this question tests.** **Seeking guidance** well means working a hard problem yourself long enough to understand it, then asking the right person at the right moment, with the groundwork that makes their help fast. The job description pairs independence with knowing when to escalate, that is, to take a problem to someone with more knowledge or authority (goal.md). In a client team, both extremes cost something: a consultant who never asks puts the client at risk, and one who asks at every step uses up the team's time. The interviewer listens for judgment: the signal that told you to ask, what you had already tried, and how you put the question.

Four signals make asking the right move, and naming yours makes the story:

- **The timebox ran out** — you set yourself a limit, such as half a day, and progress stopped before it.
- **The decision was not yours** — it commits the client's data, money, security, deadline, or scope, which someone else owns.
- **Someone already knows** — a colleague, another team, or the client has solved it before, so an hour of their time saves days of yours.
- **Being wrong is costly** — a mistake would be hard to undo, such as a data deletion or a production change.

**How it is asked:**

- "Tell me about a time you were stuck on a complex problem. What did you do?"
- "When have you escalated an issue, and how did you decide to?"
- "Describe a time you asked for help. Looking back, was it the right moment?"

**Questions to find your story:**

- When was I stuck, and what made me stop and ask?
- Which problem needed knowledge only one person had: a senior engineer, another team, the client?
- When did I find something outside my authority: a risk to the client's data, budget, deadline, or security?
- What did I bring when I asked: what I had tried, what I suspected, the options?
- What did the answer change, and what did I do with it afterwards?
- Have I waited too long to ask? A story with that lesson works too.

**A strong pick** shows effort before asking, a clear signal, a prepared question, and you carrying the fix through afterwards. If the asking was the whole of your action, the story shows dependence.
