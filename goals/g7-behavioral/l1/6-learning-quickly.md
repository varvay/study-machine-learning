---
title: Learned a new technology quickly
---

**What this question tests.** **Learning quickly** means becoming productive in an unfamiliar technology under time pressure, and applying it to real work. Finishing a course alone does not show it; delivering something with the technology does. The job description asks for continuous learning (goal.md), and a consultant often joins a client whose stack, the set of languages and tools its systems run on, is new to them. The interviewer listens for your method: what you read first, what you built to test your understanding, whom you asked, and how soon you delivered something with it.

**How it is asked:**

- "Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology quickly."
- "How do you get up to speed on an unfamiliar codebase or tool?"
- "What's something you learned recently and applied?"

**Questions to find your story:**

- Which tool, language, or framework was new to me when a project needed it?
- How long did I have, and what did I have to deliver with it?
- What was my method: the official docs, a small prototype, an expert, the source code?
- What did I misunderstand at first, and how did I find out?
- What did I build with it, and is it still in use?
- Did I pass the knowledge on: a guide, a talk, a template the team reused?

If work offers no story, this study plan can: learning Spring AI, a Java framework for building LLM applications (G6.2), and fine-tuning, further training a model on your own examples (G4.6), to build the capstone by the interview is learning under a deadline, applied.

**A strong pick** has a real deadline, a method you can describe, and something built and used at the end.
