title: Delivered end-to-end independently
---

Each story is written in STAR form with a measurable result (G7.1 L2). At L3 it is told aloud in under 2 minutes, and it holds up under **follow-up questions**: the questions an interviewer asks after your answer to get more detail on your approach, and to check the story is yours.

**Delivery in under 2 minutes.** Conversational English runs at about 150 words a minute, and slower when presenting, so 2 minutes holds roughly 250–300 spoken words. The L2 illustrative examples run about 170–190 words, 70–80 seconds aloud; that size leaves room for the headline and the learning. Split the time by the STAR weights, with the Action taking most of it:

| Part | Time | What goes in |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | ~5 s | One sentence: what you did and the result. For the L2 illustrative example: "I built and shipped a support assistant that closed 28% of tickets without escalation." |
| Situation | ~20 s | Two or three sentences of context, ending on what made it hard. |
| Task | ~10 s | Your responsibility, in one sentence. |
| Action | ~65 s | Three to five decisions, each with its reason. |
| Result | ~20 s | The number with its baseline, the effect on the client, one line of learning. |

The headline goes first so the listener knows where the story is heading; if they cut you short, they already have the point. After the learning, stop, and offer more: "I can go deeper into how I built the evaluation, if that's useful." Pausing to ask whether the interviewer wants more context is advice Amazon gives its own candidates.

**Rehearsing:**

- Speak from the L2 bullet sheet, never from a memorized script.
- Record and time each story. Over 2:15, cut the Situation first, since background is the easiest part to overtell.
- Retell it several times with different wording until the order holds without the sheet.
- Keep a 30-second version: the headline, one decision, the result. It serves when the interviewer asks for several stories in a row.

**Three follow-ups every story must survive** (plan.md):

- **"What would you do differently?"** — name one specific decision you would change, and why. "Nothing" reads as no reflection; a list of regrets undoes the story.
- **"What did the team think?"** — name a real view, including dissent, and how you learned it: a review, a retro (the team's meeting to review what went well and badly), direct feedback.
- **"What did you learn?"** — a practice you still use, and a later occasion when you used it.

**The consulting lens.** goal.md reads the job description as a consulting or delivery organization. Where it is true, thread three things through every story:

- **Client impact** — tie the Result to what the client gained or avoided, next to the technical metric.
- **Autonomy with escalation** — show what you decided alone, what you took to a lead or the client, and why.
- **Team discussion** — show how you worked with others: reviews, disagreements, handovers.

**Where this story goes wrong:**

- **A tour of the tech stack.** The interviewer scores decisions; name each tool only with the reason you chose it.
- **"We" throughout.** Your part disappears into the team's.
- **No end.** A story that stops at "it worked in testing" shows building, not delivery. End on real use.
- **Independence told as isolation.** Never asking anyone reads as a risk in a client team; one line on where you checked with someone helps (G7.2).
- **A long Situation.** Forty seconds of background leaves too little for the Action.

**In an interview** — likely follow-ups, and what a strong answer does:

- **"How did you know it was done?"** — Name the acceptance criteria, the conditions the work had to meet to count as done, that you agreed at the start, who agreed them, and the check you ran against them. It shows you defined done yourself.
- **"Where did you get stuck, and what did you do?"** — Give one specific moment, what you tried, and when you pulled someone in. It answers the autonomy-and-escalation question before it is asked.
- **"What happened after you handed it over?"** — Say who owns it now, how it is monitored, and whether it needed rework. Delivery includes what happens after release.
- **"What did the client think?"** — Give their reaction in their terms: a decision they took, such as funding the next phase, or a comment they made.
