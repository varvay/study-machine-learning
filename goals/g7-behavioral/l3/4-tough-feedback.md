---
title: Gave or received tough feedback
---

The interviewer uses this story to judge self-awareness, and how you behave in a team discussion under pressure. Tone counts as much as content: the way you describe the other person is the way the interviewer expects you would describe them.

**Delivery.** State the feedback early, close to its own words, so the listener feels its weight. Spend the Action on what you did with it: the check, the change, the follow-up. When you gave the feedback, the SBI structure (G7.4 L2) is the Action's spine. The headline carries the change; for the L2 illustrative example: "A senior engineer told me my eval number was wrong; she was right, and I rebuilt the test set before the client demo."

**Where this story goes wrong:**

- **Feedback that isn't tough.** "They said I care too much about quality" is a strength in disguise and gives nothing to score.
- **A villain.** The other person was wrong, rude, or incompetent. Describe their view fairly, even where you disagreed.
- **No change.** A received story that ends with "I took it on board" shows nothing.
- **Public or surprise feedback, told as a strength.** When you gave it, where and when you said it are part of what is scored.
- **Non-technical.** goal.md asks for technical feedback; a story about meeting etiquette misses the role.
- **A broken relationship with no reflection.** If it went badly, say what you would do differently.

**In an interview** — likely follow-ups, and what a strong answer does:

- **"How did you feel when you heard it?"** — Name the reaction honestly and briefly, then what you did instead of acting on it.
- **"Was the feedback fair?"** — Say what was right in it. If part was wrong, say how you raised that calmly, with evidence.
- **"How did they take it?"** (when you gave it) — Their reaction, how you listened to their side (the intent step of SBII), and your follow-up.
- **"Tell me about a time it went the other way."** — The other direction in 30 seconds, prepared in advance.
- **"How do you give feedback to someone more senior?"** — In private, specific, with evidence, and framed on the goal you share, such as the client's outcome.

Source: [Center for Creative Leadership — SBI and SBII](https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/closing-the-gap-between-intent-vs-impact-sbii/)
