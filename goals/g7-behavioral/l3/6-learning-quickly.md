---
title: Learned a new technology quickly
---

The interviewer tests two things: your learning method, and whether you know where your new knowledge stops. The method matters more than the technology named: the next client may run a tool you have not met yet.

**Delivery.** Spend the Action on the method, in its order: what you read first, the small thing you built to test your understanding, the mistake it caught, whom you asked. Leave out the feature tour of the technology. The headline carries speed and use; for the L2 illustrative example: "I learned Spring AI and MCP in ten days and built the tool layer for a client demo that won the next phase."

**Where this story goes wrong:**

- **A course as the result.** A certificate shows study; the role wants something built and used.
- **No time pressure.** "I learned Python over a year" is learning, but it isn't quick.
- **Overclaiming.** Two weeks in, you are not an expert; a technical follow-up exposes the gap within a minute.
- **The technology instead of the learning.** Describing what the framework does, when the question is how you learned it.
- **No mistake along the way.** A path with no wrong turn sounds untested.

**In an interview** — likely follow-ups, and what a strong answer does:

- **"How did you know you understood it well enough to use for a client?"** — Name the test: something you built and ran on real data, a review by someone who knew it, a check against the docs.
- **"What are the limits of what you know about it now?"** — State where your knowledge stops, plainly. Expect technical questions on the technology next; if the story is Spring AI, prepare G6.2.
- **"What would you do differently learning it again?"** — One change to the method, such as building the throwaway prototype on day one.
- **"How do you keep up with changes in AI tooling?"** — A concrete routine: release notes for the tools you use, one small build per new tool, and notes you keep, such as this journal.
- **"How did you share what you learned?"** — A guide, a template, or a session for the team, and whether they used it.
