---
title: Knew when to seek guidance
---

In a client team, the timing of an escalation is a risk the interviewer probes directly. Escalate late, and the client meets the problem first; escalate everything, and you cannot be left to work alone. The story must show where you put the line, and why.

**Delivery.** Spend the Action on the decision point: what you tried, the signal, and what you brought. The expert's answer takes one sentence; what you did with it takes two. The headline names the judgment; for the L2 illustrative example: "I found personal data in a training set two days before the run, settled what I could, and took the one decision that wasn't mine to my lead the same day."

**Where this story goes wrong:**

- **The helper becomes the hero.** The story ends with "and then the senior engineer fixed it", and your judgment disappears.
- **No effort before asking.** Asking at the first obstacle reads as dependence.
- **A late escalation told as a success.** If you waited too long, say so and give the lesson.
- **A vague signal.** "It felt right to ask" gives nothing to score. Name the signal: the timebox, the authority, the risk, or the person who knew.
- **Blame.** Criticizing the person who could not help, or who should have warned you.

**In an interview** — likely follow-ups, and what a strong answer does:

- **"How long did you try before asking, and how did you decide?"** — Give the actual timebox and the signal. Show the limit was set in advance, or that you recognized a boundary of authority or risk.
- **"How do you decide what is your call?"** — State a rule: "Decisions that are reversible and inside my scope, I make and report. Decisions that commit the client's data, money, security, or deadline, I take to their owner with options and a recommendation."
- **"What would have happened if you hadn't asked?"** — The concrete cost to the client or team, stated calmly, without drama.
- **"Have you ever escalated too early or too late?"** — A short second example or an honest admission. It shows you calibrate.
- **"What did your lead think of how you handled it?"** — Their actual reaction, and any change in how they delegated to you afterwards.
