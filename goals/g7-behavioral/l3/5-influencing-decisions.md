---
title: Influenced a team decision
---

The interviewer probes how you treated the people who disagreed: whether you persuaded with evidence, listened, and committed once the decision was made. Amazon names this **disagree and commit**: challenge a decision respectfully when you disagree, then commit wholly once it is decided. In consulting, the same story predicts how you will recommend an option to a client who prefers another.

**Delivery.** Give the other side's reasons in their strongest form before your own; it shows you listened, and makes your win worth more. Spend the Action on the evidence and how you presented it. The headline carries the decision and its effect; for the L2 illustrative example: "I convinced our team to use pgvector over a dedicated vector database with a one-day benchmark, which saved the client a security review."

**Where this story goes wrong:**

- **Winning as the point.** The story shows you were right and the others wrong, and nothing about how you worked with them.
- **Opinion without evidence.** "I felt strongly" and "I pushed hard" describe persistence.
- **No account of the other side.** Without their reasons, the interviewer cannot tell a debate from a disagreement you ignored.
- **A trivial decision.** Naming or formatting shows no influence on direction.
- **The client left out.** The decision's effect on the client's cost, risk, or timeline is the strongest result.

**In an interview** — likely follow-ups, and what a strong answer does:

- **"What was the strongest argument against your view?"** — State it fairly, as its owner would. Then give why the evidence still favored yours, or the point you conceded.
- **"What if the team had decided the other way?"** — Say you would commit fully and help make it work, and name the signal that would justify reopening it.
- **"Have you ever been wrong in a case like this?"** — A short, honest instance, and what you changed.
- **"How would you convince a client who wants the other option?"** — Lay out the trade-offs in their terms (cost, risk, time), offer a small proof, and put the recommendation in writing. The decision stays theirs.
- **"What did the people who disagreed think afterwards?"** — Their actual view later, such as working together on the follow-up.

Source: [Amazon — Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles) ("Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit")
